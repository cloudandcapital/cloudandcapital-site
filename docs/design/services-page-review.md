# Services: simple and specific

Diana's direction is a short, calm page that explains exactly what she helps with. The approved homepage supplies the colors, typography, close arrows, rounded cards, and small 3D icons. Avoid repeating the offer in a large hero graphic, service menu, question headlines, process cards, and promotional sections.

## Current structure

1. A text-only introduction and one contact link.
2. Three service cards. Each gives one short explanation and one takeaway. Starting inputs, full deliverables, limits, and worked examples live under Scope & details.
3. A short credibility note with Diana's portrait, finance degree, prior Morgan Stanley experience, and cloud/FinOps credentials. The same two image-only featured badges used on the homepage sit below it.
4. Three native FAQ disclosures: fit, cost, and whether new software or AI is needed.
5. One contact section with the fixed scope/quote/timeline promise, a direct email action, and an optional guided email-draft form.

Technology Cost Review, Workflow Review, and Tools & Automation retain their established anchors and legacy targets. The AI subscription/extra-usage scope, evidence limits, implementation boundaries, and independent use of review recommendations remain intact. The worked examples, free tools, worksheet, and learning pages still exist; the Services page does not need another gallery promoting them. The cost example has two contextual links to the cloud-bill and AI-plan walkthroughs.

The guided inquiry keeps its required fields, mailto generation, copy success/recovery, and direct email fallback. Its wrapper is a native disclosure so visitors can email directly or choose help composing a message. Learning retains the default form appearance.

Keep the directory and tool audits in the current docs/tool-quality-plan.md. Do not use an older PR's open status as the current tool-review verdict. Diana's next manual Claude review is the new Lens controlled-test workflow and matching guide.
