## Copy/paste prompt

Please independently review my Cloud & Capital tool and its companion walkthrough. Be candid and specific. I want to know whether this helps someone make progress on a real technology spending decision, and whether the guide matches the tool.

Tool: https://cloudandcapital.com/interactive-lab
Guide: https://cloudandcapital.com/insights/using-interactive-lab

Cloud & Capital is my business. I have a finance background and cloud/FinOps credentials, and I build tools and workflows to help people understand technology costs and save time. My voice is casual, clear, and smart without sounding corporate. The cream, sage, clay pink, and serif design is intentional. Please preserve that direction.

Start by trying the tool WITHOUT reading the guide. Then read the guide and follow it from the beginning. The tool should make sense on its own; the guide should add a useful example and explain what to do with the result.

If you can only read the pages and cannot submit the form, run JavaScript, inspect a phone layout, or use Copy/Print, say that clearly. Separate what you actually tested from a text review or inference. Do not claim an interaction worked without observing it. Use only fictional information for testing.

Please cover:

1. First visit: Who is this for? Is the input and output clear? Does a visitor understand what a decision brief is and why it would help? Is any wording needlessly technical?

2. Actual usefulness: Is the result specific enough to act on? Does it compare the choices, identify missing information, and give useful next steps? Does it add value over a generic AI chat, or just make advice look polished? Explain what would make someone come back.

3. Test these three cases, with at most one additional generation if needed to reproduce a failure:

- Choose the Software renewal starter. It has a $2,880 annual plan, 12 seats, 10 active users, a 20% higher quote, and a renewal in 30 days. Seat-pricing rules, migration effort, replacement price, and the next contract term are unknown. The calculated increase is $576 and the new total is $3,456 if the increase applies to the whole annual charge. Check whether the brief preserves the choices and uncertainty rather than inventing savings or contract rights.
- Choose AI build or buy. The current API costs $40K per month over a one-year comparison horizon; replacement and in-house costs are unknown. Check the $480K annual run rate and its assumptions. It is not proof of savings, a commitment quote, or evidence that building in-house wins.
- Start fresh with a technology decision whose options, prices, requirements, and constraints are unknown. Fill all four answers honestly with what is missing. Does it ask for the right information rather than make a confident purchase recommendation?

Also check Back/Edit answer retention, example labels after partial edits, Reset/Try another, Copy, and Print if your environment supports them. Inspect the remaining starter cases without generating extra briefs. Flag awkward wrapping, spacing, mobile overflow, or unclear disabled controls.

4. Trust: Distinguish the supplied context, the app's calculations, and the AI's suggestions. Watch for invented terms, unsupported claims about cost or disruption, and confidence that exceeds the evidence. Are privacy and saving limitations understandable? Do not treat a valid schema or correct arithmetic as proof that all the prose is correct.

5. Guide: Follow every instruction against the current interface. Is the example understandable, is the screenshot faithful, and is the arithmetic right? Does it answer “okay, what do I do with this?” Is it too long or repetitive? Cut jargon and filler while keeping my voice. Flag any feature it promises that you cannot find.

6. Alternatives: If you can browse, compare one or two relevant alternatives using their current official pages. Include a normal AI chat with a structured prompt as a baseline. Explain where this Lab has a useful reason to exist and where it falls short. Cite sources and distinguish observed capabilities from marketing. Please do not propose a large feature list just because competitors have more features.

Return: a short visitor verdict; what you actually tested and could not test; a prioritized table of findings with exact location/input, observed behavior, expected behavior, severity, and recommended fix; suggested wording for the most important copy changes; and a recommendation of ready to list, ready with small fixes, or hold. Tell me what is already working and should stay. Please do not redesign the homepage, change my positioning, or recommend accounts, courses, memberships, or a platform rebuild.
